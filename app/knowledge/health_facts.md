# Fever
A fever is a body temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher. It is usually a sign the immune system is fighting an infection. In adults, rest and fluids are often enough. Seek care for fevers above 103°F, lasting more than 3 days, or with severe symptoms. In babies under 3 months, any fever needs immediate medical attention.

# Blood Pressure
Blood pressure is the force of blood against artery walls. Normal is around 120/80 mmHg. High blood pressure (hypertension) is 130/80 or higher and often has no symptoms. It raises the risk of heart disease and stroke. Lifestyle changes and medication can manage it.

# Headache
Most headaches are tension-type and harmless, caused by stress, dehydration, or lack of sleep. Seek urgent care for a sudden severe headache, headache with fever and stiff neck, or one following a head injury. A headache lasting several days should be evaluated by a doctor.

# Common Cold
The common cold is a viral infection of the nose and throat. Symptoms include runny nose, sore throat, cough, and congestion, usually resolving in 7-10 days. Rest, fluids, and over-the-counter remedies help. Antibiotics do not work on viruses.

# Dehydration
Dehydration happens when the body loses more fluid than it takes in. Signs include thirst, dark urine, fatigue, and dizziness. Drink water regularly. Severe dehydration, especially in children or the elderly, requires medical attention.
